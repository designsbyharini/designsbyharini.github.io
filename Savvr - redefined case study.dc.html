TRY 01 · BUILT IT, DROPPED IT
“People overspend because they can't see where the money went.”

So I designed the analytics home. Categories, trends, a weekly digest. Eight screens, and it looked like a real product.

Then I asked five people to use it to decide on the flight. Every single one left the screen to work out what was still due.

TRY 02 · THE ONE THAT STUCK
“People overspend because nothing tells them what's already promised.”

One number that looks forward, with the subtraction on show. Everything after this point comes from that one sentence.

Analytics didn't die. It moved to its own screen, where looking backwards belongs.

09 · THE DECISIONS THAT MOVED IT

I won't walk you through every exploration. Three of them changed the product.

01

Put the split inside the card, not behind a link.

The first card was a number and a label. People liked it and wouldn't act on it. V02 added a subtitle, V03 added a "view breakdown" link. Both talked about the context. Neither showed it.

BEFORE · V03
Safe to spend
₹21,400
View breakdown →
→
AFTER · V05
SAFE TO SPEND
₹21,400
₹28,600 committedof ₹50,000
WHY

Five out of seven testers asked how it was calculated before they'd say if they trusted it.

COST

The split has to be right at a glance now, not just right in total.

Promising transparency isn't transparency. The proof had to be on the home screen, not one tap from it.
02

Drop the notification feed and rank two things instead.

V03 had twelve events, equal weight, newest first. Complete, defensible, and it handed the hard part straight back to the user. Cutting it meant the app had to have an opinion, and be wrong in public sometimes.

BEFORE · V03

2 things need you
01 · Credit card₹4,800
Due tomorrow. It won't pay itself.
02 · Electricity₹1,600
An estimate. Final amount lands Oct 4.
For information · 4 more
WHY

The complaint I heard most was people muting their bank app completely.

COST

Get the ranking wrong and it fails louder than a plain list would.

Hiding things is only a feature if you can explain each one you hid. That's why every item carries its reason.
03

Kill the scroll and let destinations carry the weight.

By V04 the home was three screens tall and every section could be defended. Instead of trimming copy, I made a rule: one job per screen, nothing scrolls. Anything long became a place you go to.

11 · THE LOOK

A bank should feel calm, not cheerful.

Greyscale everywhere else, so one number can be the loudest thing on the screen.

Graphite

Mint
#D0EE4A
Grey
#6B6B68
Paper
#F4F4F2
Amber
#8A4B08

Black does the work: buttons, headline cards and anything that needs you. Neon only marks safe to spend and the numbers that come from it. No gradients anywhere, so every surface is one flat colour.
