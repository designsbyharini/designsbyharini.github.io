1Problem

People started opening an account on their phone and left before finishing. They didn't know how long it would take or what to have ready, picked an account before knowing if they qualified, retyped what the ID system already had, and hit failures that never said why.

2What I found

Typing a number was easy. Proving identity is where people got stuck. In East Africa, 1 in 4 selfie and ID checks were rejected in 2024, many of them real people with a bad photo.

3What I designed

What you'll need, up front. Four named stages. Every failure gets its own screen: what happened, whose side it's on, tries left, another way through.

4Why it matters

Every drop-off has already cost the bank an ad, SMS codes and paid ID checks. Helping people recover keeps that spend working, without loosening a single check. In the first quarter after launch, completion went up about 30%.

01Context

At a branch, someone tells you what went wrong.

In shortPeople were dropping off before finishing. In the app, nobody is there to explain what to do, and that gap is what I followed.

Techurate builds onboarding for banks like Azania, Exim and ZICB. They already had a flow. My brief: help more people finish it on their phone, whether opening a new account or bringing an existing one onto the app.

The ask

It's a lot to hand to an app

Your money, your national ID number and a photo of your face, given to a screen with nobody watching.

At the branch

Someone checks it with you

A staff member looks at your ID, tells you what's missing, and sorts out problems on the spot.

In the app

You're on your own

Step after step, and one message for every failure. This gap became the thread I followed.

Version 3 · from my Figma file

Then, a bolder visual direction.

Big type, blue and lime, and a form that reads like a sentence: "I go by the name… I was born on…". The idea came from a reference I'd saved, the last frame on the right.

What I kept, what I moved on from

The sentence form stayed and became "Is this you?". The flat 3D shapes went. They looked bold but didn't tell you anything about the step you were on.

"Which number feels right?" became the SIM pickerthe sentence-style form started here →
The one I kept

In the final round, I made every screen explain its own step.

One object per step hints at what it does: a phone for the SMS code, a lens for the selfie, a card when the account opens. You know where you are before reading a word.

08Solution

It starts with "Here's what you'll need."

In shortSay what's needed up front. Give every failure its own cause, a next step and saved progress.

How long it takes and what to have ready, before anything starts. Then four named stages:

1Phone→2Identity→3Details→4Security

The ID system fills in what it already knows: less to type, less to get wrong.

Before · wrong code

"Invalid OTP. Please try again." No cause, no tries left, no other way out.

After · wrong code

Says what didn't match, how many tries are left, and points to the newest SMS.

Try it yourself

Pick an ID and tap through. Turn on failures to see how each one recovers.

For the customer

Fewer moments of "did I do something wrong?". Every failure says what happened and what to do next, and nothing typed is lost.

For the bank

More of the marketing, SMS and ID-check spend ends in an open account. Fewer accounts pushed to the more expensive branch route.

What I would validate first

Whether people really mix up the SMS code and the PIN, and whether the sentence-style details form reads faster than a plain one. Both are still hypotheses.

10Looking back

The failure screens did more work than the happy path.

In shortI couldn't stop systems from failing. I could decide what people see when they do.
01

Using the flow as a customer taught me the most

I know banking apps well, and I still got stuck. Writing down every decision and feeling showed me the problem wasn't one screen. It was not knowing what was going on.

02

My first instincts were the weakest

"Cut steps" and "add a progress bar" felt obvious. The evidence said effort per step and an honest time estimate mattered more. Writing my beliefs down is what let me catch that.

03

Design for what you don't own

I couldn't stop the ID system or the SMS network from failing. I could decide what the customer sees when they do.
